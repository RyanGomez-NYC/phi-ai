Compliance & the managing organization's responsibility

The regulatory frame the AI-native platform is built around — rule by rule, with what the software enforces mapped against what remains, explicitly and unavoidably, the responsibility of the organization that runs it. Because the platform was designed for AI use of protected data from the first line, the compliance posture is architecture, not policy wrapped around an add-on. This page is design-intent documentation, not legal advice.

2. HIPAA, in depth

Minimum necessary (§164.502(b)) is implemented at four layers: role design (the deliberate asymmetries — the Administrator cannot open a chart, the Auditor holds only audit access, Psychotherapy grants nothing else); tool design (the AI cannot read past the person it serves); sweep confirmation; and withholding with counts — visible minimization, not silent truncation. Psychotherapy notes (§164.501, §164.508(a)(2)) are a physically separate store, reachable by one role, excluded from every export, individually audited on every access. Accounting of disclosures (§164.528): the trail stores date, actor/recipient, object reference and purpose for every disclosure-class event, and the ROI service stores included and withheld counts on each production — the raw material for the six-year accounting; the workflow is the organization's. Security Rule (§164.312): access control, audit controls, integrity and transmission security are the platform's technical safeguards; §164.308's administrative safeguards and §164.310's physical safeguards are organizational. Breach (Subpart D): individuals within 60 days of discovery, HHS within 60 days (or annually under 500), media over 500 in a jurisdiction; the trail answers "what did this account touch, when, under what claimed purpose" in one query.

3. 42 CFR Part 2

Records of federally assisted SUD programs carry Part 2 handling: withheld from ordinary retrieval, and any export whose record set contains Part 2 content refuses without Part 2-specific consent for that disclosure — the refusal recorded as a first-class outcome. The 2024 final rule aligned enforcement with HIPAA and permitted TPO redisclosure under a single consent in defined cases while keeping SUD counseling notes under a separate consent; the platform's separate-store and specific-consent design matches the strictest reading, and counsel decides where the newer flexibilities are taken.

4. Information blocking, state law, FDA

  • Info blocking: every withholding is rule-based, recorded and visible — never an unexplained absence — which is exactly the showing the exceptions require; configuring withholdings without a basis is the organization's risk to avoid.
  • State law: where stricter than HIPAA, stricter wins: the consent gate is keyed on jurisdiction and modality for recording consent. Audio itself is PHI — voice is one of the eighteen HIPAA identifiers — so the ambient pipeline treats a recording like any record: encrypted under the deployment's keys, role-gated, every transcription an audited model interaction, raw-audio retention a governed setting, and the speech model self-hosted so no third party ever touches the audio (no ASR vendor BAA exists; the drafting model runs under the organization's existing AI-provider agreement). The hiv and reproductive categories exist so condition-specific statutes have something to attach to.
  • FDA CDS: the four §520(o)(1)(E) criteria are engineering constraints here — no device signals, professional-grade information, recommendations not directives, and an independently reviewable basis through citations. Deployment choices can forfeit the posture; keeping inside it is an organizational duty.

5. The managing organization's responsibilities, in full

The software is a tool. The organization that deploys it holds the legal duties. It cannot discharge these on the organization's behalf:

  1. Security risk analysis and management — documented, periodic, covering the whole environment; deploying this software is an input, never a substitute.
  2. Business associate agreements — platform operator, model provider (with no-training terms verified), hosting, and every downstream recipient.
  3. Access governance — in your identity source of truth, not here. This system is not the source of truth for users or roles: accounts, role assignments, recertification and deprovisioning are governed by the organization's identity provider and directory, and the platform enforces what that source asserts — who holds which role, reviewed on a schedule, removed the day it is no longer needed. The Control panel's matrix is the review artifact.
  4. Audit review — the chain preserves the record; someone must read it, with escalation for denial patterns, system.* overrides and export refusals.
  5. Consent capture — Part 2 consents, psychotherapy authorizations, recording consent, data-sharing agreements. The platform evaluates consent state; it cannot create it.
  6. Clinical accountability — every signed note is the signer's; AI drafting moves no responsibility off the licensed professional.
  7. Incident response and breach notification — the trail is evidence; the program is the organization's.
  8. Patient rights operations — access, amendment, restriction, accounting, on the organization's timelines.
  9. Retention and disposition — the schedules the retention screen enforces, set with counsel; disposition is a recorded human decision.
  10. AI oversight — validation against local data before clinical use, drift and subgroup monitoring, and an oversight structure with authority to pull the switches the Control panel deliberately provides.
