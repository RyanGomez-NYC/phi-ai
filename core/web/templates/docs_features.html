Feature documentation

Every feature of the AI-native platform: what it is for, who uses it, how the workflow runs, and the guardrails and audit events wired into it. Every feature here was designed for AI use of protected data from the start — the governance is in the feature, not wrapped around it.

1. Clinical workspace

1.1 PHI AI assistant (PHI RAG)

  • For: cited answers and drafts from the record.
  • Workflow: the question travels with the asker's roles and purpose; the assistant plans retrieval against its role-scoped tools; the gates release what the role allows, withholding the rest with counts; the model drafts from the released excerpts; the answer renders with citations and the withholding statement. Prompt history and saved prompts store questions only — an answer is regenerated from records, never cached as a shadow chart.
  • Guardrails: grounding contract, minimum-necessary confirmation before sweeps, counts-never-lists, the psychotherapy boundary.
  • Audits: every question and every tool read.

1.2 Patients & charts

Search and the storyboard chart, with sensitivity callouts stating what was withheld from the view and why. Every open is a phi.disclosure-class event carrying reader and purpose.

1.3 Summarization, inbox triage, ambient documentation, patient instructions

Generated working material, each with the same discipline: every sentence traceable to a cited record; triage reorders and never removes; ambient documentation runs a self-hosted ambient listener (faster-whisper, MIT, registered in the registry's ambient slot) behind the jurisdiction-aware consent gate — audio never leaves the deployment, so no ASR vendor BAA exists; transcription audits as model.inference, drafting flags ASR-uncertain terms for the signer; ambient documentation begins at the consent gate; instructions derive only from the visit's actual orders. All of it is draft until signed.

1.4 Signature queue

the signature rule as a screen. Drafts carry their full text and citations into a per-draft review; the sign control appears only after the full draft, and the refusal on the unwritable row comes from the real Epic write-surface assertion (core/governance/writeback.py), not from copy. Signing writes signature.committed; nothing advances by timeout, default or batch approval.

1.5 Document intake

Scanned-document ingestion: OCR to patient-linked FHIR resources with the original stored alongside — OCR is lossy and the scan remains the source.

2. Revenue & records — every feature's core is a model

Each feature below is powered by a registered model in the Control panel's registry, with its own enable switch, its metrics on the Model monitoring screen, and the standard degradation rule: a disabled core means the feature says so and computes nothing unmanaged.

2.1 Claims & billing — the denial risk model

The core (registry: Denial risk model, predictive, slot claims): every pending claim is scored 0–99 before adjudication from the payer and service-line denial history in the Data Store. Each score is advisory and each factor cites the real counts it came from ("this payer: 100 of 1,230 denied"). The screen leads with the scored pending queue and the strongest payer × service denial clusters — where process fixes beat resubmission. Guardrails: a score never blocks a claim; calibration and drift are watched on Model monitoring; subgroup behavior belongs to the fairness discipline.

2.2 Prior auth & appeals — the evidence assembler

The core (registry: Prior auth evidence assembler, retrieval, slot priorauth): the payer's criteria are checked by retrieval — the diagnosis, the order, the result series, the training note — each criterion citing its chart entry, gaps shown as gaps and never inferred. From that evidence the assembler drafts the appeal letter: NOT-MET criteria acknowledged plainly, and the draft lands in the signature queue where the signature rule holds it until a clinician signs. Drafting is audited (ai.query, ai.draft_created).

2.3 Documentation gaps — the coding integrity model

The core (registry: Coding integrity model, classifier, slot coding) reads in both directions: charts whose own results support a diagnosis absent from the problem list (A1c ≥ 6.5 with no diabetes coded; eGFR < 60 twice with no CKD; sustained hypertensive pressures with no hypertension), and money billed with no clinical note in a ±45-day window. A tool that only finds revenue is upcoding with better branding; both directions is documentation integrity. Findings carry their evidence and become drafted clinician queries in the signature queue — the model never changes a code or a note.

2.4 Release of information — the requirements validator

The core (registry: ROI requirements validator, classifier, slot roi): before any release, every request is validated against the configured requirements of the applicable jurisdiction — federal authorization elements (signature, expiration, scope specificity, revocation language, Part 2 consent) plus the state rules counsel configures. Verdicts are met / UNMET / needs-review, each with its citation; a hard unmet requirement makes Fulfil a recorded refusal naming the requirement (roi.refused); every evaluation is recorded (roi.requirements_evaluated). The asymmetry is the design: the validator can block a release and can never approve one — the human decision remains the release. Patient-access requests are evaluated under the access right, not the authorization rules; unconfigured jurisdictions fail closed to human review.

2.5 Sensitive categories — the classifier, in the open

The core (registry: Sensitivity classifier, classifier, slot segmentation) classifies every incoming condition, medication and document at the ingestion door — and its uncertainty is managed in the open: classifications resting on thin evidence queue for human review, uncertain stays withheld (fail closed), and every confirm/clear decision is audited (segmentation.reclassified). A missed Part 2 record is the most consequential error this platform could make, which is why the review queue is the feature, not a footnote.

3. Population

Cohort builder runs on the live OMOP layer and returns counts, never lists (the counts-never-lists rule is structural — the query path cannot enumerate). No-show risk and scheduling are registered predictive/optimization models under the fairness screen; the permitted intervention is support, never denial of scheduling. Ingest & mapping QA queues low-confidence terminology mappings for humans. Holdings and Reports answer "what is in this platform right now" with live counts and integrity checks.

4. Integration

4.1 Source & target EMRs

Connection configuration with each vendor's real seams in view — auth method, bulk capability, write surface. Saving writes config.changed.

4.2 Bulk import manager

FHIR Bulk Data runs with per-run detail (kickoff → poll → download → encrypt-store-index) and the watermark rule: only a clean run advances the last-known-complete marker. A kickoff inside the vendor's 24-hour window is refused and the refusal recorded (bulk.refused).

4.3 Streaming data

ADT, results and 835 remittance on checkpointed partitions; a gap between checkpoint and latest offset is surfaced with its exact range and never interpolated.

4.4 Bulk export manager

Records leaving in bulk, with per-run detail: psychotherapy notes never export; Part 2 refuses redisclosure without consent; sensitive exclusions are counted; a destination without a bulk write surface refuses with the seam named. Outcomes are complete / failed / refused, all first-class (export.completed / export.refused).

4.5 Streaming export

Outbound feeds under acknowledgment discipline: held means queued and accounted, retried without regenerating; paused is a governance decision from the Control panel that accumulates and does not leak.

5. System — the Control panel & model monitoring

The System Administrator's seat (system:admin, the wildcard role's screen): the model registry — every model of every kind, LLM, predictive, classifier, optimization, mapper, with bring-your-own registration by provider/model ID or HTTPS endpoint and an audited registered → enabled → activated lifecycle per capability slot; the assistant model panels with the retrieval and live-calls switches; live controls (bulk hold release, feed pause/resume); a live audit-chain verification on every load; the full role/permission matrix; and the editable platform configuration. Every switch writes a system.* event under the administrator's own name.

Model analytics & monitoring: every registered model carries live metrics computed from the stores and the audit trail — performance (score separation, calibration, acceptance rates), drift recognition (population-stability index of each model's input mix against its baseline, ≥0.10 watch / ≥0.25 act), and alerting whose alerts never auto-close: acknowledging is an audited human action and a recurring condition re-fires. Monitoring does not validate a model clinically; the organization's oversight duties stand.

6. Governance

Registry & preflight: no AI capability runs unregistered; preflight checks declarations against the egress evidence per cloud. Fairness screen: subgroup performance shown, not averaged away. Ambient consent gate: deny-by-default, jurisdiction- and modality-keyed, from the live gate (core/governance/consent_gate.py), every evaluation audited. Source attributes and EMR conformance: provenance and verified vendor capability. Audit: the trail with chain verification. Retention: schedules and the disposition queue — the platform never deletes on its own authority.

7. The refusal, everywhere

The platform's signature feature is the one it shows most often: the recorded refusal. A 403 that names its missing permission, an export history with a refused row, a rate-limited kickoff, a withheld-records count on a chart — each is the governance working in the open. If the refusals ever stopped appearing, that would be the finding.
